How ClipToAction works

You see a good reel, save it, and never look at it again. This turns those reels into a notebook you can actually search and learn from.

1. Send it a link

That is the whole of what you do. On a phone, tap Share on the reel and pick ClipToAction — the same way you would share to WhatsApp. On a computer, paste the link into the box at the top.

You never type a title, a note or a category. Everything else is worked out for you.

2. It listens to the video for you

The reel is fetched and the speech is written down in English — translated as it goes, so a reel spoken in Hindi or Hinglish comes back in English rather than in the words that were said. That is deliberate: written down as spoken, mixed Hindi and English comes back as nonsense. An AI then reads that and pulls out what was actually said. While that happens the clip shows where it has got to:

  • Waiting to be picked up — saved, nothing has started yet.
  • Being watched now — it is fetching and listening.
  • Written down — no summary yet — the words are in, the summary is not. See step 3.
  • Ready — done, open it.
  • Could not be read — something went wrong, and it says so rather than pretending.

A reel someone else already saved is not fetched twice — you get their result straight away.

3. Choose who does the summarising — one setup step

The summaries are not done on our account. Each reel is read once and shared with everyone who saved it, so the account that pays for a reading is the one belonging to whoever saved that reel first. Yours pays for what you find first. There are two ways to set this up, and one of them costs nothing and needs no account:

  • Connect an AI account. Google Gemini and Groq both give these away free. Open Settings, pick one, and it shows you exactly where to get it and what the catch is. After that every new clip is summarised on its own.
  • Do it yourself — no account needed. Open a clip and press Copy the question. Paste that into whatever AI you already use — ChatGPT, Gemini, Claude, anything. Copy its whole reply, paste it back into the box, and save. Same result.

Until you pick one of those, reels are saved and written down but nothing gets summarised.

Reels you saved before connecting an AI are not summarised on their own — open one and press Summarise it now. It is one at a time on purpose, so a free daily allowance is never used up without you asking.

4. What you get on each clip

  • What it said — a few sentences, so you can tell in seconds whether it is what you were looking for.
  • The main points — the actual steps, numbers and tactics.
  • Worth checking before you act on it — the claims the video makes, each marked by how much to trust it. Red means treat it with caution. Reels are full of confident nonsense; this is there to catch it.
  • Things to look into — the tools and terms it named, for when you want to go deeper.
  • Everything that was said — the full words, if you want them.
  • Your notes — what you thought. This is what turns a saved reel into something of your own.

5. Finding it again

The search box looks inside everything — the summary, the full words of the video, and your own notes. So you can search for something you half-remember hearing, not just a title.

Mark clips Keeping, Done or Archived to keep the list under control. An archived clip drops out of the main list, and searching still finds it — it comes back marked archived so you can tell.

6. Put it on your phone

Open this page in your phone's browser, then use the browser menu and choose Add to Home screen. That is what puts ClipToAction in the share menu, so you can send reels to it straight from Instagram or Facebook.

Worth knowing

  • Your notebook is kept on your device as well, so it still opens and reads when you have no signal.
  • Signing out clears that copy from the device.
  • Free AI accounts usually allow the provider to read and learn from what is sent to them. Settings repeats what each provider's own page said when it was last checked — where it says nothing, nobody has checked, not that the answer is no.
